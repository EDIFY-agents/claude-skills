# Trademark Policy

The **LICENSE** governs the code. This document governs the *name*. They are
separate grants, and the licence deliberately does not include this one.

"EDIFY", the EDIFY logo, and the `edify` command name are trademarks of EDIFY.

We keep this short and permissive on purpose. The point is not to police
language — it is to make sure that when a developer types `pipx install
edify-cli`, they get software we built and can support.

---

## You do not need to ask

- Say that your project uses, supports, integrates with, or is built on EDIFY.
- Write tutorials, blog posts, talks, courses, and videos about EDIFY, using
  the name in the title.
- Use the name in a comparison, a review, or a benchmark — including an
  unflattering one. Accurate criticism is nominative fair use and we would
  rather you published it than asked us first.
- Ship a plugin, skill entry, template, or integration named
  `edify-<something>` or `<something>-for-edify`, provided it is clear you are
  not us.
- Use the logo, unmodified, to link to this repository or to edify.dev.
- Quote the docs with attribution.

## You do need to ask

- Naming a **product, company, service, or domain** with EDIFY as the leading
  element — `edify.io`, "EDIFY Cloud", "EDIFY Enterprise", "EDIFY Pro".
- Distributing a **modified build** of EDIFY still called EDIFY. Fork freely;
  rename before you ship. This is the one that matters: a broken fork under our
  name generates support load we cannot answer and trust we cannot recover.
- Merchandise, event names, or user-group names using the marks.
- Any use that implies endorsement, affiliation, certification, or partnership
  that does not exist.

## Never

- Registering EDIFY, or anything confusingly similar, as a trademark, company
  name, or domain in any jurisdiction.
- Modifying the logo — recolouring, adding elements, or combining it with
  another mark.
- Using the marks in a way that suggests we authored something we did not,
  particularly anything security-relevant.

---

## Why a source-available licence has a trademark policy at all

Because they close different holes. The licence stops someone selling EDIFY
back to the market as a competing product. The trademark stops someone
confusing your users about who wrote the thing on their build server. A project
that relies on only one of the two is protected against half of what actually
happens.

Renaming a fork is a small courtesy that costs an afternoon. Answering
support requests for someone else's build is not.

## Asking

Open a [discussion](https://github.com/EDIFY-agents/edify_public/discussions) or email **legal@edify.dev**. Requests
that amount to "we want to be clear we are not you" are approved as a matter of
routine.
